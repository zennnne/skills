# Glossary

Fixed renderings for [`report-thai-business`](SKILL.md). Edit freely — a term your team renders differently is a one-line change here, and this file wins over the model's own preference.

## Allow-list — stays in English

These read as English to Thai audit professionals. Keep them in Latin script inside the Thai text.

`PwC` · `walkthrough` · `COSO` · `KPI` · `KRI` · `SOP` · `ITGC` · `ERP` · `SAP` · `GRC` · `SOX` · `IIA` · `ISO` · `Three Lines Model` · `IT` · `RPA` · `dashboard`

Deal and capital-markets vocabulary, for risk and transaction commentary. Thai professionals use these in English:

`M&A` · `private credit` · `direct lending` · `capital stack` · `preferred equity` · `mezzanine` · `loan-to-value` · `refinancing` · `due diligence` · `portfolio`

Three standing exemptions, listed or not:

- **Proper nouns and personal names** — company names, `Khun Siriporn` → `คุณศิริพร` when the Thai spelling is known, otherwise leave the roman spelling and raise a `❓ รอยืนยัน` note.
- **Product, vendor, and system names** — `Excel`, `Power BI`, `Oracle`, `Workday`. Never transliterate these.
- **Half-English compounds** keep the allow-listed part and translate the rest: `GRC module` → `โมดูล GRC`, `IT Security Policy` → `นโยบายความมั่นคงปลอดภัยด้าน IT`.

Everything else gets translated, and each unlisted term goes one way or the other — never both:

- **The term has a Thai rendering a senior reader would recognise** — use the Thai alone. `purchase-to-pay` → `กระบวนการจัดซื้อจนถึงการชำระเงิน`, `shared service centre` → `ศูนย์บริการร่วม`.
- **Thai professionals say the English term out loud** — keep the English alone and treat it as allow-listed for that document. `private credit`, `capital stack`, `M&A` are this kind of term.

The failure mode this rule exists to prevent is a **gloss** — the English trailing its own Thai in parentheses, `สินเชื่อภาคเอกชน (private credit)`. A senior client reader takes it as a sign the translator was unsure of the term, so the Thai reads hedged where the English read decisive. When the call between the two readings is close, pick one, write it clean, and name the term in an `ℹ️ ตีความว่า:` note — a term that keeps returning through that note belongs in the allow-list above.

A gloss is something **you** add, so the rule reaches only your own parentheses. Parentheses already in the source are content — mirror them where the author put them and translate what sits inside: `the Company (the “Group”)` → `บริษัท (“กลุ่มบริษัท”)`. When the source uses them to introduce an abbreviation it reuses later — `Chief Financial Officer (CFO)`, `delegation of authority (DOA)` — keep the Latin abbreviation so the later mentions still resolve: `ประธานเจ้าหน้าที่บริหารด้านการเงิน (CFO)`.
